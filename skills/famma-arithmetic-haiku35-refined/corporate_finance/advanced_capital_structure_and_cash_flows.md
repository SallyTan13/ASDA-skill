## Pattern 1: WACC with Target Capital Structure Weights

**Description:** Calculate Weighted Average Cost of Capital using target capital structure ratios (debt-to-equity, short-term/long-term debt ratios) rather than current market values. Target ratios must be converted to capital structure weights before applying component costs.

**When to Use:** 
- Question mentions "target capital structure," "target debt-to-equity ratio," or "target weights"
- Given ratios like D/E, ST/LT debt ratios instead of dollar amounts
- Asked to calculate WACC "using target structure" or "at target weights"

**Procedure:**
1. Identify target ratios: debt-to-equity (D/E), and any sub-ratios (e.g., ST debt/LT debt)
2. Convert D/E ratio to weights:
   - Weight of Equity = 1 / (1 + D/E)
   - Weight of Total Debt = D/E / (1 + D/E)
3. If debt has sub-components (ST and LT), split the total debt weight:
   - Let r = ST/LT ratio
   - Weight of ST Debt = (r / (1 + r)) × Weight of Total Debt
   - Weight of LT Debt = (1 / (1 + r)) × Weight of Total Debt
4. Calculate WACC = Σ(Weight_i × Cost_i × (1 - Tax_Rate)^(if debt))
5. Verify weights sum to 1.0

**Worked Example:**
**Question:** A company has a target debt-to-equity ratio of 0.6 and a target short-term/long-term debt ratio of 0.2. The cost of equity is 14%, cost of short-term debt is 6%, cost of long-term debt is 8%, and the tax rate is 35%. What is the WACC using target capital structure weights?

```python
# Given target ratios and costs
target_debt_to_equity = 0.6
target_st_to_lt_debt = 0.2
cost_of_equity = 0.14
cost_of_st_debt = 0.06
cost_of_lt_debt = 0.08
tax_rate = 0.35

# Step 1: Convert D/E ratio to weights
weight_equity = 1 / (1 + target_debt_to_equity)
weight_total_debt = target_debt_to_equity / (1 + target_debt_to_equity)

# Step 2: Split debt weight into ST and LT components
weight_st_debt = (target_st_to_lt_debt / (1 + target_st_to_lt_debt)) * weight_total_debt
weight_lt_debt = (1 / (1 + target_st_to_lt_debt)) * weight_total_debt

# Step 3: Calculate WACC
wacc = (weight_equity * cost_of_equity + 
        weight_st_debt * cost_of_st_debt * (1 - tax_rate) + 
        weight_lt_debt * cost_of_lt_debt * (1 - tax_rate))

# Verification: weights should sum to 1.0
assert abs(weight_equity + weight_st_debt + weight_lt_debt - 1.0) < 0.0001

wacc  # Result: 0.1025 or 10.25%
```

**Common Bugs to Avoid:**
- Using current market values instead of converting target ratios to weights
- Forgetting to apply tax shield to debt costs: use Cost × (1 - Tax_Rate)
- Not splitting debt weight when multiple debt types exist
- Calculating D/E weight as D/E directly instead of D/E / (1 + D/E)

**CHECK Steps:**
- Verify all weights sum to 1.0: weight_equity + weight_debt_components = 1.0
- If question provides target ratios, DO NOT use market values from balance sheet
- Ensure tax shield only applies to debt components, not equity

## Pattern 2: Cash Flow from Assets (CFA)

**Description:** Calculate Cash Flow from Assets, which represents the total cash generated by a firm's assets available to investors (creditors and shareholders). This is distinct from net cash flow and uses the formula: CFA = Operating Cash Flow - Net Capital Spending - Change in Net Working Capital.

**When to Use:**
- Question asks for "cash flow from assets," "cash flow to investors," or "free cash flow to the firm"
- Given income statement, balance sheet data, and capital expenditures
- Need to determine cash available to all investors (debt and equity holders)

**Procedure:**
1. Calculate Operating Cash Flow (OCF):
   - OCF = EBIT + Depreciation - Taxes
   - OR OCF = Net Income + Depreciation + Interest Expense
2. Determine Net Capital Spending (NCS):
   - Use GIVEN capital expenditures if provided
   - OR NCS = Ending Net Fixed Assets - Beginning Net Fixed Assets + Depreciation
3. Calculate Change in Net Working Capital (ΔNWC):
   - ΔNWC = (Ending CA - Ending CL) - (Beginning CA - Beginning CL)
   - OR ΔNWC = ΔCA - ΔCL (change in current assets minus change in current liabilities)
4. Calculate CFA = OCF - NCS - ΔNWC

**Worked Example:**
**Question:** A firm has EBIT of $8,500, depreciation of $1,200, and a tax rate of 40%. The firm purchased $2,350 in new fixed assets. Current assets increased by $900 and current liabilities increased by $450. What is the cash flow from assets?

```python
# Given data
ebit = 8500
depreciation = 1200
tax_rate = 0.40
capital_expenditures = 2350  # Given directly
change_current_assets = 900
change_current_liabilities = 450

# Step 1: Calculate Operating Cash Flow
taxes = ebit * tax_rate
ocf = ebit + depreciation - taxes

# Step 2: Net Capital Spending (use given capex)
net_capital_spending = capital_expenditures

# Step 3: Change in Net Working Capital
change_nwc = change_current_assets - change_current_liabilities

# Step 4: Calculate Cash Flow from Assets
cash_flow_from_assets = ocf - net_capital_spending - change_nwc

cash_flow_from_assets  # Result: 3814
```

**Common Bugs to Avoid:**
- Using net change in fixed assets from balance sheet when capital expenditures are explicitly given
- Confusing CFA with simple net cash flow (sources minus uses)
- Forgetting to subtract BOTH capital spending AND change in NWC
- Calculating OCF incorrectly (must add back depreciation and subtract taxes)
- Including financing cash flows (dividends, debt payments) in CFA calculation

**CHECK Steps:**
- If capital expenditures are explicitly stated, use that value (don't calculate from balance sheet changes)
- Verify OCF calculation includes depreciation add-back and tax subtraction
- Ensure ΔNWC = ΔCA - ΔCL (increases in CA reduce cash, increases in CL provide cash)
- CFA should equal Cash Flow to Creditors + Cash Flow to Stockholders

## Pattern 3: Cash Flow from the Firm (Investment Perspective)

**Description:** Calculate cash flows FROM the firm to measure actual cash outflows for capital investments and working capital needs. This represents the firm's investment activities and equals Capital Expenditures + Increase in Net Working Capital.

**When to Use:**
- Question asks for "cash flows from the firm" (not "to the firm")
- Need to determine total investment cash outflows
- Analyzing how much cash the firm is deploying into assets

**Procedure:**
1. Identify Capital Expenditures (purchases of fixed assets)
2. Calculate Change in Net Working Capital:
   - Increase in NWC = cash outflow (firm investing in working capital)
   - Decrease in NWC = cash inflow (firm releasing working capital)
3. Cash Flow from Firm = Capital Expenditures + Increase in Inventory + Increase in Other Working Capital Items
4. Alternative: Sum all investment-related cash outflows

**Worked Example:**
**Question:** A firm purchased fixed assets for $27,000 and increased inventories by $2,300. Accounts receivable increased by $1,500 and accounts payable increased by $800. What are the cash flows from the firm?

```python
# Given investment activities
purchases_fixed_assets = 27000
increase_inventories = 2300
increase_accounts_receivable = 1500
increase_accounts_payable = 800

# Step 1: Capital expenditures
capital_expenditures = purchases_fixed_assets

# Step 2: Change in Net Working Capital
# Increases in current assets are uses of cash (outflows)
# Increases in current liabilities are sources of cash (inflows)
change_nwc = increase_inventories + increase_accounts_receivable - increase_accounts_payable

# Step 3: Cash Flow from Firm (total investment outflows)
cash_flow_from_firm = capital_expenditures + change_nwc

cash_flow_from_firm  # Result: 29300
```

**Common Bugs to Avoid:**
- Confusing "cash flow from firm" with "cash flow to firm" or net cash flow
- Netting sources and uses when question asks specifically for outflows
- Forgetting that increases in current liabilities offset working capital needs
- Including financing activities (debt, equity) in investment cash flows

**CHECK Steps:**
- Verify you're calculating OUTFLOWS (investments) not net cash flow
- Increases in current assets = cash outflows (uses)
- Increases in current liabilities = cash inflows (sources that reduce net outflow)
- Result should be positive for firms making investments

## Pattern 4: Equity Issuance Pricing and Book Value Impact

**Description:** Calculate the impact on book value per share when new equity is issued. The issuance price depends on the context: market price when transaction is market-based (e.g., "same P/E ratio"), or book value when transaction is book-based.

**When to Use:**
- Question asks for "new book value per share" after equity financing
- New investment or acquisition is equity-financed
- Given phrases like "same P/E ratio," "market-based transaction," or "at book value"

**Procedure:**
1. Determine issuance price:
   - If "same P/E ratio" or market-based: use current market price per share
   - If "at book value" or book-based: use current book value per share
2. Calculate new shares issued = Investment Amount / Issuance Price
3. Calculate new total shares = Old Shares + New Shares
4. Calculate new total book value = Old Book Value + Investment Amount
5. Calculate new book value per share = New Total Book Value / New Total Shares

**Worked Example:**
**Question:** A company has 65,000 shares outstanding with total book value of $5,300,000 and market price of $75 per share. The company makes a $1,500,000 equity-financed investment that has the same P/E ratio as the firm. What is the new book value per share?

```python
# Given data
old_shares = 65000
old_total_book_value = 5300000
market_price_per_share = 75
investment_amount = 1500000

# Step 1: Determine issuance price
# "Same P/E ratio" means market-based transaction
issuance_price = market_price_per_share

# Step 2: Calculate new shares issued
new_shares_issued = investment_amount / issuance_price

# Step 3: Calculate new total shares
new_total_shares = old_shares + new_shares_issued

# Step 4: Calculate new total book value
new_total_book_value = old_total_book_value + investment_amount

# Step 5: Calculate new book value per share
new_book_value_per_share = new_total_book_value / new_total_shares

new_book_value_per_share  # Result: 80.00
```

**Common Bugs to Avoid:**
- Using book value per share when question indicates market-based transaction
- Using market price when question indicates book value transaction
- Forgetting to add investment amount to total book value
- Calculating new shares at wrong price (mixing market and book values)

**CHECK Steps:**
- If "same P/E ratio" or "market valuation": use market price for issuance
- If "at book value" or no market reference: use book value per share for issuance
- Verify: New Total Book Value = Old Book Value + Investment Amount (always true)
- New book value per share can be higher or lower than old, depending on issuance price

## Pattern 5: Implied Synergy Valuation from Merger Terms

**Description:** Determine the implied synergy value that an acquirer believes exists based on their willingness to proceed with a merger at specific terms. This involves calculating the premium paid and understanding what synergy value justifies that premium.

**When to Use:**
- Question asks "what must [acquirer] feel is the value of synergy"
- Given merger terms: exchange ratio, stock prices, P/E ratios
- Need to determine IMPLIED synergy from transaction structure
- Reconciling merger decision with apparent premium paid

**Procedure:**
1. Calculate market value of target company (shares × price per share)
2. Calculate total consideration paid by acquirer:
   - If stock deal: Exchange Ratio × Acquirer Share Price × Target Shares
   - If cash deal: Cash Amount
3. Calculate premium paid = Consideration - Target Market Value
4. Implied synergy value = Premium paid (minimum synergy to justify deal)
5. Alternative: Synergy = Combined Market Value - (Acquirer MV + Target MV)

**Worked Example:**
**Question:** Company A (acquirer) has 2 million shares at $50/share. Company B (target) has 500,000 shares at $30/share. Company A offers 0.8 shares of A for each share of B. What is the implied synergy value that justifies this merger?

```python
# Given data
acquirer_shares = 2000000
acquirer_price = 50
target_shares = 500000
target_price = 30
exchange_ratio = 0.8

# Step 1: Calculate standalone market values
acquirer_market_value = acquirer_shares * acquirer_price
target_market_value = target_shares * target_price

# Step 2: Calculate consideration paid
new_shares_issued = exchange_ratio * target_shares
consideration_paid = new_shares_issued * acquirer_price

# Step 3: Calculate premium paid
premium_paid = consideration_paid - target_market_value

# Step 4: Implied synergy (minimum to justify premium)
implied_synergy = premium_paid

# Alternative calculation: what combined value is implied
implied_combined_value = acquirer_market_value + consideration_paid
total_standalone_value = acquirer_market_value + target_market_value
implied_synergy_alt = implied_combined_value - total_standalone_value

implied_synergy  # Result: 5000000 (or the premium amount)
```

**Common Bugs to Avoid:**
- Calculating synergy as combined earnings minus sum of individual earnings (equals zero)
- Confusing accounting synergy with market value synergy
- Not recognizing that premium paid implies minimum synergy belief
- Ignoring the exchange ratio in stock-for-stock mergers

**CHECK Steps:**
- If acquirer pays premium, implied synergy ≥ premium (otherwise irrational)
- Verify consideration calculation uses acquirer's share price (what target receives)
- For stock deals: New shares issued = Exchange Ratio × Target Shares
- Synergy can be expressed as market value increase or as premium justification
