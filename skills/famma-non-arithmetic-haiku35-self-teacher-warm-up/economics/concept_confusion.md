## Pattern: Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) Directional Misinterpretation

**Description:** Misunderstanding the relationship between inflation differentials and exchange rate movements, specifically confusing the direction of exchange rate changes when comparing inflation rates between two countries.

**When to Use:** Triggered by keywords: purchasing power parity, exchange rates, inflation comparison, currency valuation

**Procedure:**
1. Identify the inflation rates for the domestic and foreign countries
2. Compare the inflation rates (domestic vs. foreign)
3. Apply the PPP principle: Higher domestic inflation relative to foreign inflation means the domestic currency is expected to WEAKEN
4. For an exchange rate quoted as DOM/FOR, weakening of the domestic currency means the RATE WILL RISE
5. Verify the direction by checking: If domestic inflation > foreign inflation, expect exchange rate to increase

**Example (sanitized):**
> **Scenario:** Compare inflation rates between Country A (3%) and Country B (2%)
> **Wrong approach:** Incorrectly assuming the exchange rate would fall
> **Correct approach:** Recognize that higher domestic inflation implies currency depreciation, causing the exchange rate to rise

## Pattern: Monetary Policy Independence Misconception

**Description:** Incorrectly assessing the degree of monetary policy autonomy based on incomplete understanding of exchange rate regimes and their constraints on central bank actions.

**When to Use:** Triggered by keywords: exchange rate regime, monetary policy, central bank independence

**Procedure:**
1. Identify the exchange rate regime (fixed, floating, restricted, unrestricted)
2. Assess the level of capital flow restrictions
3. Evaluate the central bank's ability to set independent monetary policy
4. Understand the hierarchy of monetary policy constraints:
   - Floating exchange rate: Maximum policy independence
   - Unrestricted fixed exchange rate: Moderate constraints
   - Restricted fixed exchange rate: Minimal policy independence
5. Verify by analyzing the specific mechanisms that limit monetary policy choices

**Example (sanitized):**
> **Scenario:** Comparing monetary policy options for countries with different exchange rate systems
> **Wrong approach:** Oversimplifying the relationship between exchange rate regime and policy autonomy
> **Correct approach:** Carefully analyze the nuanced constraints on monetary policy for each regime

## Pattern: Real Bond Yield Comparative Analysis Trap

**Description:** Incorrectly assuming similarity or comparability of real bond yields across different economies without thoroughly examining underlying economic factors.

**When to Use:** Triggered by keywords: real bond yields, inflation-adjusted returns, cross-country yield comparison

**Procedure:**
1. Extract nominal bond yields for each country
2. Calculate expected inflation rates for each country
3. Compute real bond yields by subtracting expected inflation from nominal yields
4. Do NOT assume automatic similarity between countries
5. Consider additional factors:
   - Currency valuations
   - Economic growth expectations
   - Monetary policy differences
6. Verify by examining multiple economic indicators, not just inflation

**Example (sanitized):**
> **Scenario:** Comparing bond yields between two developed economies
> **Wrong approach:** Assuming real yields are automatically similar
> **Correct approach:** Carefully decompose yields, considering multiple economic factors
