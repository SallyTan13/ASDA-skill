## Pattern: Debt Value Calculation in Merger Scenarios

**Description:** Misunderstanding how debt value is calculated in a merger, treating debt as a static nominal amount instead of a contingent claim dependent on the combined firm's total value.

**When to Use:** Merger analysis questions involving debt, scenarios with multiple economic states or conditions

**Procedure:**
1. Identify the total firm value across different scenarios/conditions
2. Subtract outstanding debt claims from the total firm value
3. Recognize that debt value is proportional to the firm's value, not a fixed amount
4. For merged entities, sum the values from each original firm before calculating debt claims
5. Verify that debt value changes based on the combined firm's total value

**Example (sanitized):**
> **Scenario:** Two technology companies merge, with different firm values under various market conditions
> **Wrong approach:** Keeping debt at original fixed amount regardless of combined firm value
> **Correct approach:** Calculate total firm value across scenarios, then determine debt claims proportionally

## Pattern: Stockholder Cash Flow Comprehensive Calculation

**When to Use:** Financial statement analysis, cash flow to shareholders questions

**Procedure:**
1. Identify total cash returned to shareholders
2. Include both:
   - Direct dividends paid
   - Net changes in equity (stock repurchases/issuances)
3. Calculate the comprehensive cash flow by summing these components
4. Cross-check balance sheet equity changes
5. Verify total cash returned matches calculated amount

**Example (sanitized):**
> **Scenario:** A manufacturing company with complex equity transactions
> **Wrong approach:** Reporting only dividend payments
> **Correct approach:** Calculate total cash returned by combining dividends and net equity changes
